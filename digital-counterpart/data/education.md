# Education

## BSc Artificial Intelligence at Johannes Kepler University Linz

I am studying Artificial Intelligence at Johannes Kepler University Linz (JKU). I chose JKU because of its reputation in technology and AI, its connection to researchers such as Sepp Hochreiter, the flexibility of the programme, distance-learning options, industry connections, and Austria's educational and technical environment. Linz also appealed to me because of its growing AI and technology ecosystem.

Before university, most of my technical learning came from building things myself. I had already spent years working with robotics, web development, automation, and machine learning. The degree is now helping me build the mathematical and theoretical foundations beneath things I had previously learned mainly through experiments and projects.

One university project involved satellite pattern classification. It gave me practical experience with the steps involved in improving and optimizing machine-learning models. I had trained neural networks before university, but formal study has helped me understand the process more systematically.

I enjoy both the theoretical and practical sides of the degree. Mathematics and computer science have long been among my strongest academic areas, and I am especially interested in turning mathematical ideas into working intelligent systems.

Academic grades are intentionally not included in this resource.

## Budhanilkantha School and Cambridge International A Levels

I attended Budhanilkantha School in Kathmandu from Grade 5 in April 2016 through June 2024. I completed Nepal's Secondary Education Examination (SEE) in 2022, then continued into Cambridge International A Levels through June 2024.

I entered the school through a competitive Grade 5 entrance examination. Roughly 1,000 students sat the examination that year. Budhanilkantha is a boarding school, so living and studying there from a young age helped me become more independent, disciplined, resilient, responsible, and better at managing my own time.

Mathematics and Computer Science were my strongest and most enjoyable A-Level subjects. I also studied Further Mathematics for one year but did not sit the final examination.

Outside academics, I was active in science, technology, robotics, and student leadership. I was a member of the Science, Technology and Robotics (STR) Club, served as a house prefect, and later became a school prefect during my senior A-Level year.

My science and technology competition achievements include:

- 1st place, District Level Science Exhibition, 2024
- 2nd place, District Level Science Exhibition, 2023
- Winner, 18th SET Exhibition at St. Xavier's College, Maitighar, 2023
- Best Prototype Award in Innovation and Technology, Singapore International STEM Innovation Challenge (SISTEMIC), 2023

These competitions gave me experience representing my school at local, national, and international levels.

## Robotics and makerspace learning

I started experimenting with robotics in 2017 after seeing senior students work on an Arduino-based obstacle-avoiding robot. My first robotics project was a robotic arm. There was no formal robotics platform available to me at the time, so much of my early learning came from finding components, studying independently, experimenting, and building whatever I could.

Budhanilkantha School later established a makerspace. Access to better tools and components let me work more deeply in robotics and embedded systems.

Over the years, I worked with:

- Arduino
- Raspberry Pi
- ESP32 and ESP8266
- Ultrasonic, infrared, RFID, soil-moisture, LDR, pH, DHT11, and gas sensors
- Servo motors and other motors
- Relays, resistors, and diodes
- 3D printing and laser cutting
- Computer vision
- IoT and cloud-connected systems

My builds included robotic arms, assistive technology for blind users, smart dustbins, Roomba-style robots, smart water dispensers, agricultural automation systems, and smaller experiments.

### Smart Farm and Its Ecosystem, also called the Autonomous Nursery

I started Smart Farm and Its Ecosystem at Budhanilkantha School in 2023. The project began with two people and later grew into a larger robotics group. The prototype combined embedded systems, IoT, sensors, automation, and machine learning.

The system used Arduino and Raspberry Pi, camera-based data collection, temperature and humidity sensing, soil-moisture and gas sensing, relays, water pumps, drip irrigation, filament-bulb heating, fan-based cross-ventilation, and a cloud-connected monitoring and control dashboard. The physical prototype was approximately 60 cm by 30 cm by 25 cm.

A major part of the project involved training a machine-learning model for plant-disease classification. This was one of the first times I saw how effectively a trained model could solve a physical-world problem, and it strongly influenced my decision to study AI more seriously.

## Knowledge Park robotics team

In 2023, I helped found a student robotics team called Knowledge Park. It began with a small core team and grew to around ten founding members from the AS and A-Level cohorts. We deliberately recruited students interested in robotics and technology so the group would include people with different skills and levels of experience.

The school makerspace supported us with components and resources. We worked on robotics and technology projects, with a strong interest in practical automation and agricultural applications.

The experience taught me how to organize a team, recruit people, share technical knowledge, and turn an idea into a project that other students could contribute to.

After graduating from Budhanilkantha School, I was invited back as a science teacher. I especially enjoyed spending time in the makerspace, where I voluntarily helped younger students learn and build projects.

I mentored students in:

- Python

- Electronics

Students I worked with went on to build projects including drones and a tomato-classification system. Teaching younger students reinforced my own understanding and made me enjoy explaining technical ideas as much as building them.

## Self-directed software education

A large part of my engineering background is self-taught. I started programming at around age 12 with QBASIC after seeing my older sister use it for school. From there, I learned through YouTube, blogs, experiments, documentation, and project work.

My early programming path progressed roughly like this:

QBASIC → HTML/CSS/JavaScript → React → C++ → Python → TypeScript

I also learned C-style embedded programming through Arduino development.

One of my earliest web projects was an attempt to recreate YouTube. It looked very little like the real site, but it pushed me from consuming technology into trying to build it myself.

Learning web fundamentals before relying heavily on frameworks later made it easier to understand React, Next.js, TypeScript, and full-stack development at a deeper level.

## Self-directed AI and machine learning

I trained my first machine-learning model with a friend in 2023 using TensorFlow. My early work focused on classification and convolutional neural networks, including applications connected to the Smart Farm project.

I have worked with TensorFlow, NumPy, Matplotlib, Pandas, and CNN-based classification workflows. I began working seriously with large language models in 2024 and have since experimented with models and APIs from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and open-source model families such as Llama.

My approach to prompt design is to make the task, context, constraints, and expected outcome clear enough that the model does not have to infer unnecessary details.

Today, I spend a significant amount of time working with AI coding agents and using them to build full-stack systems. I think of myself primarily as an AI builder at this stage: someone who likes turning AI capabilities into working products and automation systems while continuing to strengthen the underlying machine-learning theory.

## Current learning and long-term direction

I am especially interested in agentic AI, AI agents, AI coding agents, machine learning, intelligent automation, AI research, and full-stack AI systems.

My earlier robotics work was largely about automating physical systems. AI made automation feel much more flexible and intelligent, and that connection still shapes the kinds of problems I want to work on.

I am also interested in mathematics, economics, finance, entrepreneurship, and technology product development. I previously did graphic-design work, which contributed to my interest in how technical systems are presented and experienced by users.

In the future, I would like to become an AI researcher. AI engineering and AI consulting are also career paths that interest me. I would like to pursue a PhD and work on systems that combine research, software engineering, and intelligent automation.

Builder-first is probably the best description of how I learn: I usually understand a technology most deeply once I have tried to make something real with it. Formal university study is adding theory, mathematics, and research foundations to years of project-driven learning.
